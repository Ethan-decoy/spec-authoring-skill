# Guideline Authoring

This is an English translation of [SKILL.md](SKILL.md). The Chinese original is the primary version for maintenance and validation and takes precedence if meanings differ.

Use proportionate constraints to establish shared understanding, enabling users to make choices within a clear purpose, principles, and boundaries. The following are considerations to apply to the actual problem; they prescribe neither fixed sections for the result nor an execution order.

## Ground the guidelines in their actual use

Understand the purpose, context, scope, and actual users of the guidelines. Distinguish users or affected parties when those differences matter to the conventions.

Draw on user input, relevant material, and actual practice. When the material is sufficient, produce a draft that can be reviewed. Clarify gaps that affect the core meaning or tradeoffs, or leave them explicitly unresolved. State the assumptions needed for reasonable inferences.

## Evidence and the authority of conventions

Distinguish requirements the user has confirmed, observed conventions, facts, and new suggestions. Preserve sources, premises, and confirmation status where they affect applicability.

Retain conventions that remain valid and apply to the current goal. Observed practices are clues for judgment; they do not automatically become binding. An isolated example or a preference from another context does not automatically become a general requirement. When drawing on external guidelines, preserve the context in which they hold. Factual claims need evidence.

When conflicts arise, make their basis explicit. Incorporate tradeoffs the user has settled into the guidelines; present unsettled tradeoffs as questions or suggestions.

## Shared understanding

When names or concepts need explanation, use established terms in the applicable context and clarify their meaning, scope, and easily confused distinctions. Make the context explicit when a term has different meanings, and explain the correspondence when several terms refer to the same concept. A name alone does not establish meaning; retain uncertainty when the material is insufficient. Place necessary explanations close to their use or refer to existing conventions.

## Principles, tradeoffs, and boundaries

Give priority to the meanings, qualities, relationships, and boundaries that need to be maintained in common, together with their purpose and conditions of applicability. Use constraints sufficient to uphold shared requirements; within the purpose, principles, and boundaries, preserve as much choice of methods as possible. Broad quality expectations need a basis for judgment in the current context.

Keep explicit requirements, prohibitions, and justified precise conventions accurate. Keep method suggestions advisory, and avoid turning them into mandatory workflows, fixed steps, or output templates. Make default practices, conditional recommendations, optional approaches, and explicit boundaries distinguishable.

Keep conditions visibly connected to their conclusions, and exceptions to the conventions they qualify. Explain the reasoning behind substantive tradeoffs and points prone to misuse; describe consequences or alternatives where needed. Use examples to reveal key differences; their details do not automatically become additional requirements.

The practical benefit of a rule should justify the cost of applying and maintaining it. Retain content that changes understanding or judgment, and avoid accumulating unrelated prohibitions in response to individual failures.

## Organization and revision

Organize content for use and reference, placing related concepts, principles, conditions, and exceptions where they can readily be read together. Keep the shared basis visible, and link to details for particular cases with clear conditions for consulting them.

Give each convention an identifiable authoritative home, connecting related documents through clear responsibilities and references. Explain the role of each reference while retaining necessary explanations close to their use.

Base revisions on user intent and actual problems. Preserve unchanged conventions that remain valid, and retain terminology and phrasing suited to the current use. Explain the impact and unresolved issues when a change affects existing understanding or ways of working.

Use the location specified by the user or already established for the output; let its language and format serve its purpose. The guidelines themselves contain usable conventions. Place research, individual validation records, and notes on the current revision separately according to their purpose.

## Check the practical effect

Use real situations to check whether users can understand the conventions, recognize applicable conditions, and make tradeoffs within the boundaries, and whether the rules, examples, and related documents agree. Allow multiple concrete results to satisfy the guidelines, judging them against the purpose and boundaries. Having every section present or being able to restate principles does not establish effectiveness. Evaluate actual task performance when validating the effect, and state honestly what remains unverified.
